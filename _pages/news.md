## March 2026 - Putting Our Beam to the Test
 <img src="/images/3point.jpg"/>

As part of the EVvPres project, we carried out three-point bending tests on our prestressed concrete beams.

During the tests, we carefully monitored the applied load and the resulting displacement to capture how the beam responded as the load increased.

Another step in connecting what we simulate with what happens in the laboratory.

## February 2026 - What Does Prestress Loss Look Like?
 <img src="/images/damage.jpg"/>

What does prestress loss look like?

This image shows the end of one of our prestressed concrete beams after the anchorage was deliberately released, creating a localized loss of prestressing force.

Introducing this controlled damage state is an important part of our experimental program. It allows us to investigate how a change in prestress affects the dynamic behaviour of the beam and whether such changes can be detected through vibration-based structural health monitoring.

Sometimes, to understand how damage can be detected, we first have to create it.

## January 2026 - When Your Job Description Includes Hitting a Beam with a Hammer
 <img src="/images/hammer.jpg"/>

When your job description includes hitting a beam with a hammer, you know you're working in experimental structural engineering.

During our January testing, we used impact hammer excitation to investigate the dynamic characteristics of one of our prestressed concrete beams. By introducing a controlled impact and observing the resulting response, we can learn more about the dynamic behaviour of the structure.

It may look like we're simply hitting a beam with a hammer, but there is a lot of science behind that little impact.

Fortunately, the beam knew it was for science. 😄

## October 2025 - First Project Outcomes Published

We are happy to see the first outcomes of our project making their way into the scientific literature.

Our paper, *“Application of Sparse Autoencoder to Damage Detection of a Model Steel Truss Bridge,”* was presented by Patrícia Vaňová at the EVACES 2025 Conference and has now been published as part of the *Experimental Vibration Analysis for Civil Engineering Structures* proceedings. The paper was co-authored with Javier Fernando Jiménez-Alonso and Zhiyu Jiang. 

The study explores the use of a sparse autoencoder for damage detection in a model steel truss bridge, connecting our work in structural dynamics with data-driven approaches to structural health monitoring.

This is one of the first published outcomes of the EvPres project and an encouraging step as we continue developing our research.

🔗 [Read the paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-96106-9_36)

## October 2025 - EVPRES Research at SPACE Conference 2025
 <img src="/images/space.jpg"/>

In October, our research was presented at the SPACE Conference 2025 by Patrícia Vaňová.

The presented paper, *“Impact of Parallelization Settings on Computational Time in Modal Analysis Using FEM Software,”* looks at the computational demands of modal analysis and the influence of parallelization on the time required for large-scale numerical simulations.

Understanding these computational requirements is an important part of our work, as efficient numerical modelling becomes increasingly important when dealing with complex structural analyses and large datasets.

This study is another step towards making the computational side of the EVPRES research more efficient and practical.

## July 2025 - Material Properties Testing
 <img src="/images/material.jpg"/>

At the 28-day mark, the EVPRES team conducted a series of material characterization tests to evaluate the performance of our concrete samples.

One of the key procedures was modulus of elasticity testing. This test helps determine how concrete deforms under load — a critical parameter for both numerical simulations and modal analysis, where material stiffness directly influences dynamic response.

Accurate determination of these values ensures the reliability of our theoretical models and the validity of our experimental investigations.

Organized by Professor Zhen Sun and hosted by the School of Civil Engineering at Southeast University in China, the workshop gathered experts to discuss structural health monitoring (SHM) of girder bridges using real-time data and the current conditions of bridges in China and Slovakia.
